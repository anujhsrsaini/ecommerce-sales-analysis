# Who Actually Shops With Us — Segments for Q4 Spend

**Bottom line: put Q4 spend behind acquiring more customers who look like our top-quartile repeat buyers — 875 people (8.75% of our base) who already drive 29% of all revenue — not behind chasing volume from one-time buyers.**

## Definitions

Of our 10,000 customers, 8,438 have ever placed a paid order (1,562 have never converted at all — a separate activation problem). Among converters, I define **repeat = 2 or more paid orders**. But a raw repeat count hides two very different behaviors: some "repeat" customers order roughly weekly, while 262 customers (3.1% of converters) ordered every 2-3 days for the entire 91-day window — 20 to 178 orders each. That's not organic shopping; it's more consistent with resellers, shared accounts, or bots. I've excluded that group from the recommendation below and flagged it separately for an ops/fraud review.

## One-time vs. repeat

- **One-time buyers: 55.5% of customers, only 12.4% of revenue.** Average spend: ₹7,479 (one order).
- **Normal repeat buyers (2-19 orders, ~weekly cadence): 41.4% of customers, 57.7% of revenue.** Average spend: ~₹46,600.
- **Hyper-frequency outliers: 3.1% of customers, 30.0% of revenue** (excluded from targeting, see above).

Revenue is steeply concentrated: the top 10% of customers by spend account for 52% of revenue; the top 20% account for 70%. This isn't a base to market to uniformly — it's a base to prioritize.

## The best-customer segment

Within normal repeat buyers, the top quartile by spend — **875 customers (8.75% of our whole base)** — average **10 orders each**, spend **₹94,414 on average**, order roughly **every 7.6 days**, and last bought just **21 days** before our data ends (still active, not lapsing). That's **~12.6x** what a one-time buyer spends, and this group alone generates **29% of total revenue**.

## Recommendation

Q4 spend should go toward two things, in this order: (1) **retention and referral programs aimed at this top-quartile repeat segment** — they're proven, active, and high-value, so protecting and cloning them (lookalike audiences, loyalty perks, early access) has the clearest ROI; (2) **a modest, tested second-purchase nudge** (email/discount) for recent one-time buyers, since they're over half our base but underperform badly on revenue — worth a small experiment, not a big bet. I would *not* build campaigns around the hyper-frequency tier until we understand who they actually are — right now they look anomalous, not aspirational.
